Every game starts with an idea. Guanche Warriors started with a simple ambition: to create an original JRPG inspired by the Canary Islands.

It's a personal project, but it's also a way for me to learn game development by actually making a game, one feature and one problem at a time.

## The Vision

Guanche Warriors is an independent 2.5D turn-based JRPG inspired by the landscapes, culture and heritage of the Canary Islands.

The idea is to combine the feeling of classic JRPG adventures with an original fantasy setting and a distinctive visual identity.

I'm particularly interested in how the environments, characters and atmosphere can communicate that inspiration without simply recreating existing games.

There's still a lot to explore, and some of the more distinctive parts of the project are things I'd rather reveal when they're ready.

## From an Idea to a Prototype

When I started working on Guanche Warriors, my initial approach was a traditional 2D game with pixel art.

It felt like a natural direction for a project inspired by classic JRPGs.

The first objective wasn't to build a complete world. It was much simpler: create a character that could move, interact with the environment and begin communicating with NPCs.

Those early experiments gave me something valuable: a working foundation to test ideas rather than making every decision on paper.

As development progressed, I started reconsidering the technical approach. Moving towards a 2.5D presentation with a 3D environment opened up more possibilities for the way I wanted to handle lighting, physics and the world itself.

The visual direction is still evolving, but those early prototypes have already helped shape the project.

## Building the Game

I'm developing Guanche Warriors with Unity and C#.

So far, much of the work has focused on foundational systems:

- Character movement and exploration.
- Environmental collisions.
- NPC interactions and dialogue.
- Early gameplay prototypes.
- Visual and lighting experiments.

Some features have already gone through more than one iteration. Others are still being developed and tested.

I've also started collaborating with [carpinterzeta](https://github.com/carpinterzeta), who is contributing to the game's development.

The idea is to keep building progressively, testing each system before moving on to more complex parts of the project.

## Why a Devlog?

I want to document the journey from these early experiments to a playable demo.

This journal isn't just about showing finished results. It's also about recording the technical decisions, unexpected problems, mistakes and lessons that come with learning to develop games.

Some posts will cover Guanche Warriors directly. Others will focus on programming challenges and experiments that help me grow as a developer.

## What's Next?

For now, the focus is on strengthening the foundations of the game and gradually connecting its different systems.

There's still a long road ahead, and that's exactly what I want this development journal to capture.

Welcome to the development journey of Guanche Warriors.
